ABM Vote Simulation — Model Documentation

Agent-Based Model of Spanish vote-preference dynamics under rising immigration.
Based on “El futuro de Vox está en Getafe, no en el Barrio Salamanca” by Manuel Romero (Dec 2025).

1. Model Overview

The simulation creates a population of individual agents, each with a personal income, a birth origin (native or foreign-born), and a neighbourhood assignment. At every time step each agent votes for one of four parties via a probabilistic utility function. Between steps, new foreign-born agents enter the population, concentrating in lower-income neighbourhoods, while native incomes remain constant.

The goal is to show how rising immigration that concentrates in poor areas shifts vote shares — particularly the growth of far-right support among low-income natives.

4. Income Generation

Income is drawn from a Beta distribution, then clipped to [0, 1]:

Native-born

income = clip(Beta(2.5, 2.5) × 1.20, 0, 1)

Symmetric bell-shaped distribution (mean ≈ 0.5), scaled up by the NATIVE_INCOME_PREMIUM factor of 1.20. Effective mean ≈ 0.60.

Foreign-born

income = clip(Beta(2, 4), 0, 1)

Right-skewed distribution (mean ≈ 0.33). Foreign-born agents are concentrated in the lower income range.

This produces a realistic ~20% income gap between the two populations, matching empirical data.

5. Neighbourhood Assignment

Each agent is assigned to a neighbourhood probabilistically at creation. The probability is based on a Gaussian kernel centred on the agent's income:

Native-born

P(neighbourhoodk) ∝ exp(−3.0 × (income − nb_incomek)²)

Natives settle near neighbourhoods matching their income peers — a tight, symmetric clustering.

Foreign-born

P(neighbourhoodk) ∝ exp(−2.0 × (income − nb_incomek)²) × exp(−1.5 × nb_incomek)

Foreign-born agents also cluster by income, but with an additional penalty for high-income neighbourhoods (exp(−1.5 × nb_income)). This replicates the article's key finding: immigrants concentrate in lower-income areas regardless of their personal income.

6. Vote Preference Function

Each agent computes a utility score for each of the four parties. The scores pass through a softmax function to produce vote probabilities, and the agent draws a single vote stochastically.

P(partyj) = exp(uj) / Σ exp(uk)

The utility u for each party is the sum of four components:

Factor 1 — Personal Income

Higher income favours the right; lower income favours the left. This is the traditional class vote.

PartyUtility contributionEffect
Far-Left−0.5 + 1.0 × (1 − income)Modest boost at low income
PSOE+0.5 + 1.5 × (1 − income)Strong low-income base
PP−0.5 + 2.0 × incomeStrong high-income correlation
VOX−0.3 + 0.3 × (1 − income)Slight low-income lean (post-2019 shift)

Factor 2 — Birth Origin (Native vs Foreign-born)

Foreign-born agents lean left; native agents lean slightly right and further towards the far-right.

PartyNative bonusForeign-born bonus
Far-Left−0.2+0.5
PSOE−0.1+0.4
PP+0.2−0.3
VOX+0.3−1.0

Note the strong −1.0 penalty for VOX among foreign-born agents — they very rarely vote far-right.

Factor 3 — Neighbourhood Immigration × Income Interaction (natives only)

This is the core mechanism from the article. The effect of local immigration on vote choice depends on neighbourhood wealth:

sensitivity = max(0, 1.5 × (1 − nb_avg_income))

In poor neighbourhoods (low nb_avg_income), sensitivity is high (≈ 1.5). In rich neighbourhoods, it approaches zero — the “shield of wealth”.

PartyUtility contributionEffect
VOX+sensitivity × nb_foreign% × 5.0Large gains in poor, high-immigration areas
PSOE−sensitivity × nb_foreign% × 4.0Loses support where immigration is felt most
PP+sensitivity × nb_foreign% × 0.5Picks up a small share

This factor does not apply to foreign-born agents, who do not shift right in response to co-ethnic concentration.

Factor 4 — Neighbourhood Income (Class-Vote Reinforcement)

PartyUtility contribution
PSOE+0.3 × (1 − nb_avg_income)
PP+0.3 × nb_avg_income

Living in a poor neighbourhood further reinforces PSOE support; living in a rich one further reinforces PP. This captures peer/contextual effects beyond individual income.

7. Simulation Dynamics

At each time step the simulation performs:

  1. Compute neighbourhood statistics — average income and % foreign-born for each neighbourhood.
  2. Vote — every agent evaluates the utility function and casts a probabilistic vote.
  3. Record results — party vote shares and overall foreign-born % are stored.
  4. Add immigrants — len(agents) × FOREIGN_GROWTH_RATE new foreign-born agents are created with low incomes and assigned to (mostly low-income) neighbourhoods.

What changes over time

  • Total population grows (new immigrants each step).
  • Foreign-born share rises from ~14% upward.
  • Poor neighbourhoods become progressively more diverse.
  • Rich neighbourhoods remain largely unaffected.

What stays constant

  • Each agent's personal income (fixed at birth).
  • Each agent's neighbourhood assignment.
  • Neighbourhood income levels (structural).
  • All utility weights and model parameters.
